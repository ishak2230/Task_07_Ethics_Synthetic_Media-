# Task 7 — The Ethics of Synthetic Representation

## Project Overview

This project examines the ethical and governance issues surrounding synthetic media. It builds directly on Task 6, where I created and evaluated AI-generated audio and a synthetic avatar video.

Task 7 moves from creating synthetic media to considering how the technology should be governed. The project contains two phases:

- **Phase A:** Ethical analysis based on my Task 6 experience, including the truth, consent, context, and scale dimensions of synthetic media and an evaluation of possible mitigations.
- **Phase B:** A synthetic-media governance policy designed for a specific organizational setting.

## Organizational Context

For Phase B, I chose a **university communications office**.

A university communications office produces public-facing content such as announcements, informational videos, websites, social media posts, and promotional materials. Synthetic media could be useful for narration, accessibility, translation, and informational content.

However, university communications also depend on credibility and public trust. Synthetic content that impersonates an official, lacks appropriate consent, or is presented without disclosure could create confusion. This makes a university communications office a useful setting for developing clear rules about responsible synthetic-media use.

## Reflection

One of the most surprising things I learned from Task 6 was how quickly synthetic media could become convincing. Small adjustments to the ElevenLabs settings significantly improved the naturalness of the generated voice.

At the same time, different forms of synthetic media introduced different weaknesses. The D-ID avatar had convincing lip-sync but limited facial expressions and body movement. Hive also identified a sample of the natural-sounding audio as AI-generated with 99.1% confidence.

Working on Task 7 made me think beyond whether synthetic media looks or sounds realistic. Issues such as consent, truthfulness, disclosure, context, scale, and organizational responsibility become equally important once this technology is used outside an experiment.

The policy developed in Phase B reflects that lesson by allowing limited beneficial uses of synthetic media while establishing situations where its use should be restricted or refused.
